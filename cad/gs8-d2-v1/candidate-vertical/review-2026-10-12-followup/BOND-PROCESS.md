# Bonded-catch process development

This is a proposed coupon and per-build inspection method, not permission to fabricate or assemble an unqualified joint. The epoxy product, surface preparation, cure, printed dimensions, joint strength and warm creep remain unqualified. No unbuilt fixture receives credit in the numerical pose envelope.

## Dry trial and access

Before electronics or heat-set-sensitive assemblies are fitted, dry-fit the separate insert in the actual printed tub. Protect the exterior and support the tub inverted on its roof so assembly +Z points down toward the bench. The support must be flat and stable; it must not distort the roof or block the accessible +Y groove outlet. Inspect and clean the complete host groove from the blind pocket's +Y side to the joint-face edge. A clear CAD channel does not establish that printed material or viscous adhesive can escape through it.

Keep the tenon's -Y locating face and the axial joint-face seating lands dry and clean. Adhesive is proposed only on the deliberately clearanced +X, -X and +Y faces, with a coupon-established quantity. Do not fill the blind pocket or coat the zero-clearance datum. Do not infer an acceptable bondline or dosing volume from the groove dimensions alone.

## Seating and cure

Lower the insert axially into the upward-facing pocket. Apply a light, distributed seating load along assembly +Z, centred within the joint footprint. Do not push sideways on the projecting catch body or hang a weight from it: either can introduce a tilting moment. Maintain the chosen dry datum position without a point load on the tine. Any temporary support or fixture must first be shown on the coupon to preserve joint closure and the measured face position throughout cure; its design and load are not yet qualified.

Leave the +Y overflow outlet accessible. Remove squeeze-out before gel using the adhesive manufacturer's qualified process, without contaminating the catch face, dry datum, seating lands or nearby mechanism. If the insert cannot fully seat, stop and clean/rework before cure; do not increase force to hydraulically drive adhesive into the joint.

## Record every build

Using the same defined datums and calibrated instruments as the coupon, record both catch-face positions at multiple X/Z points and angular alignment (one centre distance cannot detect yaw or tilt), joint closure at all accessible edges, and insert depth before adhesive, before gel and after full cure. Record uncertainty. Compare the measured geometry with the final manifest-bound pose study; the study's 0.1 mm axial underseat case is a chosen screening case, not a mechanical stop or a manufactured fit allowance. Further withdrawal is physically possible through the open pocket mouth and remains a separate retention failure mode.

The 0.1 mm value bounds the chosen axial reference displacement, **not the maximum opening across a tilted joint face**. The selected compact-Kowa audit's feasible transformed poses reach approximately 0.23995 mm at the sampled joint-face points (`stub_feasibility` rows, `joint_face_gap_range_mm`). That nominal CAD opening is not a qualified adhesive thickness, seating limit or manufacturing tolerance. Establish the full measured seating and adhesive/process envelope with the actual material, preparation, cure and instrument uncertainty before accepting a build.

A feeler or printed gauge can be a useful inspection aid only after its own dimensions, access and uncertainty are verified. Do not claim that a nominal CAD gauge certifies a 0.10 mm running clearance. Test the as-built coupon and then the required complete-tub/camera/lens assembly under the separate reviewed load protocol. A local cropped coupon cannot qualify the full 0.5 N m design-torque path. Keep the adhesive SKU, batch, preparation, cure history, temperature, seating observations and failure-mode photographs with the build record.
